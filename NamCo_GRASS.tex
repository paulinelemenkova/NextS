\section{Introduction}

Lake Nam Co is the highest and the largest salt high-altitude lake among the thousands of lakes located on the Tibetan Plateau, China. Formed as a result of complex tectonic movements in Himalaya during Tertiary period, it presents now a slightly saline basin located at the elevation of 4,718 m a.s.l. with an average depth of 100 m \cite{Wangetal2009Limnology}. In the late Quaternary period, Lake Nam Co had a vast area and was connected to other small basins surrounding its surface \cite{tb00544}. Old lake terraces elevated up to 28 m above the current lake level evidence that Lake Nam Co gradually decreased in size since the Holocene due to the drying climate starting \cite{Tsui}. 

The surroundings of Lake Nam Co with Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains located in Lhasa terrane, Tibetan Plateau, has experienced rapid glacier retreat over the past 10 years. Snow and ice melting results from the climate warming which includes the rise of air and water temperatures in the region. For instance, \cite{2017JD027379} report the raise of water temperatures in lake over at an approximate rate of 0.5° C during recent four decades since 1979 and high rates of warming for air temperatures over land with 0.2° C. Warming in both mountain glaciers and surface of high-altitude lakes is observed in Tibetan Plateau as a result of global climate-environmental changes and in turn has consequences on the surrounding ecosystems and landscapes \cite{2021JD036320,LIU20216}. Glacier retreat and snow melt is a common occurrence in recent decades due to several unique regional meteorological and characteristics of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and other Tibetan highlands. For instance, changes include the downward trend of snow depth in the high altitude mountains and, in contrast, upward trend of snow depth in the plains of Tibetain Plateaus are reported by \cite{ZHU2022152491}. 

Glacier retreat and snow melt trigger geological and environmental processes in the surroundings areas. As a result of glacial meltwater, the surface area of Lake Nam Co is currently expanding and currently occupies 10,680 km \textsuperscript{2} of catchment area and a total area of 2017 km \textsuperscript{2}. Such expansion in lake's surface results from the hydrological inflow from local rivers and melting glaciers situated in the nearby areas. Thus, Nyainqen Tanglha Mountain Range, located to the south of the Lake Nam Co has a large number of glaciers which meltwater runs into the lake basin through rivers and streams \cite{ZHOU201389}. Recent studies report that glacier meltwater affects the hydrological cycle through redistributed components of water recharge and discharge on the Tibetan Plateau \cite{HE2024130556}. For example, glacier meltwater recharge takes up to 10\% of the total groundwater recharge over the Lhasa River Basin and this percentage increases \cite{CHEN2021100871}. 

Furthermore, one of the main environmental problems in Tibetan Plateau is soil erosion. Through runoff, erosion accelerates the rate of soil losses and destroys upper vegetation layer since the underlying ground surface is abraded of the rock materials, leaving only debris on the bedrock surface  \cite{LI2024107695}. Furthermore, snow and glacier melt result in deficit of surface water which becomes less available for plants and irrigation. In turn, it leads to a decrease in natural cover as the surface vegetation. 

In turn, Nam Co Lake affects local climate and weather conditions significantly through lower albedos, large heat capacities, and reduced surface roughness. Such effects in lake-air interactions largely impact the distributions of heat fluxes, cumulus clouds, and precipitation which creates local setting for moisture conditions and distribution of lacustrine types of vegetation \cite{2018JD028330}.
