\title{Biennial fluctuations of glacier extent in Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co surroundings in Lhasa terrane, Tibetan Plateau, China}

\begin{abstract}
The analysis of glacier extent and land cover changes is a key approach for understanding the dynamics of ecosystems in Tibetan Plateau. However, the links between spatio-temporal fluctuations in the extent of snow and glacier, and associated landscape changes remain elusive. Here, a series of six satellite multispectral images Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS of the selected part of the Lhasa terrane are evaluated based on technical algorithms of machine learning (ML) using Random Forest Classifier by scripting approach of GRASS GIS. The satellite images shows a prominent fluctuation in glacier coverage from 2013 to 2023, characterised by strong heterogeneities caused by climate changes. In the northern range of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains, glacier retreat is revealed from 2013 to 2017 followed by the advance in 2019 and 2021 and stabilization in 2023, which may drive the changes in vegetation coverage due to the fluctuations in water balance masses. In the southern part of the imagery close to Lhasa, the highest glacier coverage is detected for year 2019 which is suggested to be caused by significantly higher precipitation and lower temperatures in this period, which accounts for the climate changes. We propose that such glacier dynamics is reflected in the land cover types as a surface expression of hydrological balance, soil structure related to permafrost distribution and warming in air temperatures that affect vegetation through phenology cycles. The integrated applications of ML methods, scripting approach of GRASS GIS to image analysis supported by the geological and topographic investigation of the study area provides sound tools for a better understanding of the ecosystems's evolution over the selected regions of Tibetan Plateau, China.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cartographic visualization of land cover classification is often used in a variety of environmental applications. To mentioned a few of them, mapping land cover changes is useful for evaluating landscape dynamics \cite{HUANG20201039,DU2024168593,ZHAI2023110734}, agriculture monitoring and sustainable development \cite{CHEN2024111488,Qiuetal2003,info14040249,CHENG20231190}, analysing urban sprawl \cite{DING2022103001,ZHANG2023113801,ZHANG2022109715}; modelling habitats in reserved landscapes \cite{WEN2024}, applied geologic analysis \cite{LI2024100056}, classification and analysis of vegetation in context with topographic variability \cite{Lemenkova2022}, biodiversity conservation \cite{JIN2022108981,LUO2024111325} and land parcels segmentation \cite{LIU2023103265,Lemenkova202320}. Land cover mapping is also frequently used to visualise the expansion or retreat of lakes as a response to seasonal fluctuations in lacustrine environments \cite{ZhangGuoqingetal,Lemenkova202322,Lemenkova202313} assess ecosystem services \cite{Wei,Pasquarella} and assist in the land development, planning and policy \cite{Song}. In all these and similar cases, the general aim of land cover mapping it to accurately represent the landscapes and features on the Earth's surface visible from space. The challenge os this task is how to effectively evaluate the satellite data and extract meaningful information that can be then used  in landscape analysis for the thematic representation of the Earth's ecosystems.

Remote sensing data has proved to be a valuable tool for cartographic visualization and environmental monitoring of landscapes as a spatio-temporal dynamics \cite{Wangetal2016,Shaoetal2008,Lemenkova202324,FASSNACHT2015115,Lemenkova202310,LIU2021102481} and detecting land cover changes in particular \cite{Kulkarni_Bahuguna_2002,jmse11040871}. They allow for rapid and effective data extraction for monitoring and mapping changes in land cover types across diverse areas worldwide. The key approach to processing satellite images is classification which can be performed using unsupervised (automatic) and supervised methods \cite{LI2023129285,Lemenkova202229,LI2020111915}. The main idea behind these methods lies in grouping pixels on the satellite images together so that they form clusters, or groups of pixels, with similar properties of spectral reflectance. The machine algorithms recognise similarities in spectral signatures of these pixels across all the bands and assigns them into classes during classification process \cite{LI2023103351,Lemenkova202321}. Classes may be categorised into the predefined land cover types identified on the study area. Once the classification is achieved and the accuracy assessment is completed, the maps obtained on the basis of the classified images are used for comparative analysis to analyse changes that take place over the specific study area for each land cover type that has taken place between the target time periods \cite{CHEN2007298,Lemenkova202323,LIU2021104942}.

A variety of techniques and cartographic approaches may be used to process remote sensing data for mapping changes in land cover types. Among the most widely used methods is the supervised and non-supervised classification of satellite images. The land cover change analysis is then based on the comparative analysis of the classified several multi-temporal images. This method allows to analyse changes, to visualise and estimate the boundary expansion or contraction of selected landscape parcels which indicate landscape dynamics over time and space or get insights into the vegetation patterns \cite{YANG2024111657,Lemenkova202227}. 

The application of novel machine learning (ML) approaches have been proposed recently in environmental studies with aim to increase the accuracy and automation of remote sensing data processing \cite{SHEN2024101069,CHEN2024104300}. These methods are based on the use of a number of scripting algorithms that enable to process, classify and analyse data using advanced syntax of programming languages \cite{WU2023103407,Lemenkova202215,Lemenkova202203}. The use of machine learning in cartography enables to extensively use computer-based tools to assess the raster data and evaluate landscape changes accurately and automatically \cite{HAN2023104933,Lemenkova202021,WANG2024167483}. The machine learning methods use a variety of computer vision algorithms for image processing, for instance, extra trees, random forest, logistic regression, support vector machine and many more. In this study, we focus on the frequently used method of Random Forest (RF) which provides an overall classification of satellite images for change detection on satellite images as the product of ML techniques \cite{LI2017286,WANG202294,WANG2023138015,WANG2024104304}.

Using ML methods, this study investigates glacier retreat in the region of the Tibetan Plateau, China, with the special focus on the Lake Nam Co surroundings and Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains, Figure \ref{fig01}. Himalayan glaciers are important global water reservoirs which are essential factors for richness of biodiversity and ecosystems \cite{Rana,Koerner,LIU2022109097,LU2023115236}. The extended region of Hindu Kush Himalaya which includes the Pamirs, Tien Shan and Alatau Mountains plays a crucial role in the hydrology of Central Asia \cite{Bolch2019}. At the same time, they are highly sensitive to climate change and demonstrated shrinkage over the past decades. However, the rates and character of glacier retreat is still not well known with regard to regional differences. Although the most essential reason for glacier melt is caused by global warming, however, the responses to the increase in temperatures differ by regional glaciers. For example, while Himalayan glaciers are in general retreating, those located in the Karakorum, in contrast, advancing, and the glaciers in Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains are fluctuating. Moreover, strong effects of local topography on glacial and catchment morphology are reported by \cite{LOIBL201422}.

Despite the necessary geographical and environmental conditions being in place for catastrophic potential of glacier retreat across the Lake Nam Co area, regional scale studies on comparative analysis of land cover changes caused by glacier retreat and snow melt are still limited for Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains. There are also many lacustrine basins in the region of Lhasa terrain region, that do not have received attention to monitor the size or rate of glacier retreat with regard to land cover changes analysis. At the same time, investigating changes in river and lake hydrology over Tibetan Plateau is perspective using satellite remote sensing data to analyse basin-wide land cover changes. Using remote sensing imagery processed by machine learning methods, this article examines the changes in land cover types over the ten-year period of 2013-2023 in Lake Nam Co and Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains, Tibetan Plateau, China, Figure \ref{fig01}.

Complex interactions between the tectonic development and geologic structure, glacial and hydrological changes as a response to climate change and atmospheric processes, and finally, are reflected in the ecosystems over the Tibetan Plateau \cite{EHLERS2022104197}. For example, the interplay between geomorphology, sediment distribution and soil types in highland regions of Tibetan Plateau are reflected in soil development which has distinct distribution patterns as a function of sediment types and geomorphic settings \cite{YANG2020107040}. Soil types vary in response to the local climatic settings, distribution of the geomorphic units and their structural components such as floodplains, hillslopes or alluvial fans, etc. In turn, various soil types necessarily determine the distribution of vegetation types. Changes in one or more of these domains trigger related processes throughout the ecosystem of the Tibetan Plateau in regional scales. Nevertheless, the links between these systems are often poorly understood. 

Here we investigate how recent climate change in short-term dynamics (2013-2023) have impacted permafrost, glacier extent, and ecosystems on the Lake Nam Co and Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains, Tibetan Plateau. We perform this by processing remote sensing data such as satellite images using machine learning (ML) methods through GRASS GIS where we use Random Forest classifier approach to image analysis. Moreover, we compile the results with topographic, geologic and tectonic data as well as perform literature analysis using resources documenting dynamics in land cover types in southern part of the Tibetan Plateau. The emphasis of this research is on demonstrating 10-year trends in environmental changes reflected in the dynamics of land cover types and glacier extent over the region of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co. The objective is to map how highland ecosystems in Lhasa terrain surroundings have changed under the effects of the fluctuations of warmer and wetter periods in the past decade. 

\section{Study Area}

\begin{figure*}[h!]
    \centering
	\includegraphics[width=18.0cm]{Fig_03.jpg}

Lake Nam Co is the highest and the largest salt high-altitude lake among the thousands of lakes located on the Tibetan Plateau, China, which is why is it sometimes referred to as Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, Figure \ref{fig01}. Precise description on the extent and names of the geographic entities is described by \cite{LIU2022103893} who reports that the Tibetan Plateau occupies the area of $1.82x106 km^2$ with mean elevation of 4465 m, forming a part of the Pan-Tibetan Highlands. The formation of the Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains, a southern entity of the Tibetan Plateau is deeply connected to the geologic history of the Himalayan-Tibetan orogen which experienced the Indo-Asian collision at ca. 70 Ma \cite{YinHarrison}. 

Formed as a result of complex tectonic movements in Himalaya during Tertiary period, it presents now a slightly saline basin located at the elevation of 4,718 m a.s.l. with an average depth of 100 m \cite{Wangetal2009Limnology}. The highest and greatest plateau on the Earth with elevation surface above 4000 m a.s.l, the Tibetan Plateau is located on the Tibet Autonomous Region and the Qinghai province, China \cite{LiBingyuan}. Its eastern boundary correlates with the extent of the Hengduan Mts \cite{Li1987,Li1989}. The northeast corner is delimited by the Qilian Mts, the southern boundary is situated on the south foot of the Himalayas, the northern one -- on the north foot of western Kunlun Mts and Altyn Tagh fault and Qilian Mts, and the western boundary merges with Pamir, \cite{ZHANGLiZheng}. 

A complex tectonic history briefly outlined above is schematically represented in map in Figure \ref{fig02} showing current distribution of active faults and folds in the Himalayan-Tibetan orogen. In the late Quaternary period, Lake Nam Co had a vast area and was connected to other small basins surrounding its surface \cite{tb00544}. Old lake terraces elevated up to 28 m above the current lake level evidence that Lake Nam Co gradually decreased in size since the Holocene due to the starting drying climate, \cite{Tsui}. The 'Third Pole' indicated in the tectonic scheme of Figure \ref{fig02} refers to the Himalayan-Tibetan glaciers which received such name due of the amount of snow and ice volumes in Himalayas that are comparable with those reserves in Polar regions on a global scale, \cite{Bolch,Adnan}. 

Complex tectonic structure of the region is represented schematically in Figure \ref{fig03}. The mountain-building processes giving the birth to Himalayan and the Tibetan Plateau were caused by the collision of Asia and India which began in the Cenozoic with active volcanism, outward expansion and thrusting in central Tibetan Plateau \cite{XIA2011850,TaylorAn,YANG2022105152}. 

Further tectonic development the Himalayan-Tibetan orogen resulted in additional tectonic strains during Paleozoic and Mesozoic which is spatially correlated with the distributed Triassic flysch complex in the Qiangtang terranes, \cite{REN20133}. The construction of the Tibetan Plateau included regional formations as a result of crustal shortening which now forms its modern structure. Northern blocks of the Tethyan Himalaya developed during the Eocene with the Kunlun Mts formed in the west and the Qilian Mts in its eastern border, Figure, \ref{fig04}. Mantle dynamics of upper lithosphere and anisotropy of the margins of the Tibetan Plateau resulted in surface deformation which shaped heterogeneous geomorphic forms \cite{XIAO2023230046,LV2024230209}. Such setting created favourable conditions for the environmental evolution that influenced paleoclimate and is now reflected in rich variety of landscapes over the Tibetan Plateau \cite{WANG2023111775}. As a result, ecosystems over the Tibetan Plateau are notable for extreme variability and diversity. For instance, processes of ecological dynamics at regional scale of West China and rich taxonomic pattern in ecosystem of Tibetan Plateau is reported by \cite{JIA2022107703}.

The surroundings of Lake Nam Co with Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains located in Lhasa terrane, Tibetan Plateau, has experienced rapid glacier retreat over the past 10 years. Snow and ice melting results from the climate warming which includes the rise of air and water temperatures in the region. For instance, \cite{2017JD027379} report the raise of water temperatures in lake over at an approximate rate of 0.5° C during recent four decades since 1979 and high rates of warming for air temperatures over land with 0.2° C. Warming in both mountain glaciers and surface of high-altitude lakes is observed in Tibetan Plateau as a result of global climate-environmental changes and in turn has consequences on the surrounding ecosystems and landscapes \cite{2021JD036320,LIU20216}. Glacier retreat and snow melt is a common occurrence in recent decades due to several unique regional meteorological and characteristics of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and other Tibetan highlands \cite{WuZhu2008,Yaoetal2007}. For instance, changes include the downward trend of snow depth in the high altitude mountains and, in contrast, upward trend of snow depth in the plains of Tibetan Plateaus are reported by \cite{ZHU2022152491}. 

Glacier retreat and snow melt trigger geological and environmental processes in the surroundings areas. As a result of glacial meltwater, the surface area of Lake Nam Co is currently expanding and currently occupies 10,680 km \textsuperscript{2} of catchment area and a total area of 2017 km \textsuperscript{2}. Such expansion in lake's surface results from the hydrological inflow from local rivers and melting glaciers situated in the nearby areas. Thus, Nyainqen Tanglha Mountain Range, located to the south of the Lake Nam Co has a large number of glaciers which meltwater runs into the lake basin through rivers and streams \cite{ZHOU201389}. \cite{Zhangetal2013} recently reported the increased water mass in lakes in the Inner Tibet Plateau related to the increase in lake's level and rate which confirms the effects from global warming. Morerover, recent studies report that glacier meltwater affects the hydrological cycle through redistributed components of water recharge and discharge on the Tibetan Plateau \cite{HE2024130556} which favours to these processes through the geologic settings, Figure \ref{fig03}. For example, glacier meltwater recharge takes up to 10\% of the total groundwater recharge over the Lhasa River Basin and this percentage increases \cite{CHEN2021100871}. 

The effects of climate change resulted in extensive permafrost degradation over the Tibetan Plateau as a results of the rise in air temperature and notable warming. For instance, the rise in temperatures results in changes in lake evaporation which is a key component of the lake water balance \cite{2015JD024523}. Moreover, \cite{YANG201031} reports the increase in the lower altitudinal occurrences of permafrost over the last 3 decades along with the increase of mean annual ground temperatures. More prominent increase is recorded over the past 2 decades in the south where the Lake Nam Co and Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains are situated. The degradation of the permafrost and related processes of soil freezing and thawing  cause environmental deterioration. One of the main environmental problems in Tibetan Plateau is soil erosion. Through snowmelt and glacier runoff, erosion accelerates the rate of soil losses and destroys upper vegetation layer since the underlying ground surface is abraded of the rock materials, leaving only debris on the bedrock surface \cite{LI2024107695}. Furthermore, snowmelt and glacier runoff result in deficit of surface water which becomes less available for plants and direct irrigation for agricultural purposes. In turn, it leads to a decrease in natural cover as the surface vegetation. 

Nam Co Lake significantly affects local climate and weather conditions through lower albedos, large heat capacities, and reduced surface roughness. Such effects in processes of lake-air interactions largely impact the distributions of heat fluxes, cumulus clouds, and precipitation which creates local setting for moisture conditions and distribution of lacustrine types of vegetation \cite{2018JD028330}. Moreover, the hydrology of Lake Nam Co and complex topography on the surrounding terrain have important impacts on the local weather and climate \cite{2021JD036190}. Hence, the variations in hydrological balance become more essential since the lake belongs to the endorheic basin of Qinghai-Tibet Plateau \cite{Zhuetal2010}. In turn, glacier is highly sensitive to changes in air temperature where positive accumulated air temperature and precipitation level results in changes of glacier surface albedo \cite{Zhouetal2010}. Such interconnected and complex processes results in gradual changes of glacier extent, hydrological regime that affect external surface processes regarding soil and vegetation system on the Lhasa terrain.

\section{Data}

The dataset covers the area of Lake Nam Co, southwestern region of the Tibetan Plateau, Figure \ref{fig04}. The United States Geological Survey (USGS) provided the necessary satellite imagery to perform, visualise and map land cover analysis with 30-m resolution multispectral Landsat 8-9 Operational Land Imager (OLI) and Thermal Infrared (TIRS) remotely sensed images. The satellite images cover study areas of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co for six dates in cold period with high level of snow/glacier extent: 31 December 2013, 21 December 2015, 10 December 2017, 16 December 2019, 21 December 2021, 03 December 2023. The high-quality images were selected for December, which is the period with the high snow and glacier extent. These time periods reflected a significant variations in glacier and snow coverage over the ranges of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains which shows the development of land cover types after snow and glacier melt, and hydrological-environmental setting of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co, Tibetan Plateau. The dataset is shown in Figure \ref{fig05}.

The images were selected with cloud-minimised visibility for comparison to analyse landscape dynamics (all the used available scenes had low cloudiness and low distortion of visibility). The Landsat OLI/TIRS image for 31 December 2013 was used as a training polygon for retrieving the seed for machine learning. The requirement for cloudiness level was set up on lesser than 10\% for all the Landsat images. Additional thematic data used in this study include Digital Elevation Model (DEM) used for analysis of relief, the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM), the General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans (GEBCO) for topographic mapping and geological dataset mapped using vector layers. Due to unprecedentedly detailed representation of the Earth's surface, the SRTM DEM proved to be a valuable source of information for terrain analysis \cite{Huang,Lemenkova202212,YAN2024108957}

Along with the similarity in seasonal timing (winter period of December and November) necessary for comparing the differences in land cover types, the Landsat OLI/TIRS images had also already been ortho-rectified and corrected using Landsat Collection Level 1 corrections for terrain to allow for direct comparison of the imagery. All the data are predefined and projected using Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) Zone 46 for western part of the People's Republic of China (P.R.C.). Basin-wide topographic raster data in digital format were available from GEBCO, NetCDF format, available from the \href{https://www.gebco.net/}{https://www.gebco.net/} GEBCO organisational website, which provided the necessary terrain reference data for the evaluation of relief of the surroundings. The topographic map has been plotted using Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) version 6.4.0 \cite{Wessel} using scripting methods for cartographic data visualization described in earlier works \cite{Lemenkova202217,Lemenkova202213,Lemenkova202206}. Data import was performed using GDAL libraries embedded in GRASS GIS which has a wide toolset for processing geospatial setting of data including re-projection \cite{technologies11020046}.

\subsection{Colour composites}
Colour composites of the satellite image Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS were performed using combination of various multispectral bands for identification of land features performed in the GRASS GIS. technically, the command employed for creating composites is based on the 'r.composite' module of GRASS GIS as follows (example for bands 7-5-4 in image for 2013):

\begin{verbatim}
r.composite blue=L_2013_07 \
	green=L_2013_05 red=L_2013_04 \
	output=L_2013_754 --overwrite
d.mon wx0
d.rast L_2013_754
d.out.file output=Tibet_754 \
	format=jpg --overwrite
\end{verbatim}

The representation of selected colour composites is shown in Figure \ref{fig06} with the selected bands 3-4-5, 7-5-4  and 5-4-3. \subsection{Machine Learning}

Following land cover class identification using previous steps of clustering and classification and re-classification, all the six images had been reclassified using machine learning (ML) methods with a special approach of Random Forest (RF). 

This approach employs the Scikit-Learn ML library of Python programming language along with its Pandas package for complimentary data processing. The essential idea of Random Forest algorithm lies in integrating the data to average noisy yet unbiased models of random data. In this way, RF reduces the variance in dataset. Since trees can capture complex interaction in cells, they are used in RF algorithm as a key approach to data modelling and analysis. The mathematical foundation of the RF classification consists in the following steps of the workflow initially developed by \cite{598994}. 

For $b=1$ to $B$, a bootstrap sample $Z*$ of size $N$ from the training data has been drawn. Afterwards, the random-forest tree $T_b$ is increased to the bootstrapped data. This is done iteratively by the recursively repeating the logical steps for each terminal node of the tree that represent individual class in land cover classification, until the minimum node size $n_min$ is reached. The $m$ variables are selected randomly using the data obtained in 'r.random' module of GRASS GIS obtained from the pixels variables 'p'. The best variable/split-point are then selected among the $m$ and the model splits each node into two 'daughter' nodes. 

Hence, the output model of the ensemble of trees is received as $\{T_b\}_1^B$. Then, to make a prediction of the assignment of each pixel within the matrix of the raster image to the specific land cover class, the model evaluates each new point $x$ (that is, a cell on the satellite image) using the following logical expression. 

Let $\hat{C_b}(x)$ be the class prediction of the $b^{th}$ random-forest tree. Then, the RF classification is performed by running the following equation \cite{709601}:

\begin{equation}
\hat{C^B_{rf}} = majority \:  vote \:  {\hat{C_b}(x)}_1^B
\end{equation}

Hence, random forest classification obtains a class vote from each tree, and then classifies it using majority vote and analysis of each pixel within the raster image. Technical implementation of the RF classification using GRASS GIS scripts consists in the following steps. First, the training pixels have been generated from an older land cover classification in 2013 using the 'r.random' module of the GRASS GIS as follows: 

\begin{verbatim}
r.random input=L_2013_clusters seed=100 \
	npoints=1000 raster=training_pixels --overwrite
\end{verbatim}

Afterwards, these training pixels were used to perform a classification on the Landsat images for 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021 and 2023. The training of supervised approach was performed using the 'RandomForestClassifier' function in the 'r.learn.train' model of GRASS GIS and then evaluated the probability of each pixel to be assigned into the specific land cover class using the 'r.learn.predict' module of GRASS GIS as follows:

\subsection{Accuracy Assessment}

Accuracy assessment of the classification performed using by i.maxlik module evaluates the correctness of the classification the cell spectral reflectances in imagery data which is based on the spectral signature information. Technically, the code used for plotting accuracy assessment maps is presented below with a special function 'reject=': 

\begin{verbatim}
i.maxlik group=L_2013 subgroup=res_30m \
	signaturefile=cluster_L_2013 \
	output=L_2013_clusters 
	reject=L_2013_cluster_reject 
\end{verbatim}
 
 The accuracy assessment evaluates the probability of correctly classified pixels and visualises the results on a map holding reject threshold results in percentage of the correctly classified pixels. The logic underlying this method is based on the chi square test which evaluates each discriminant result at various threshold levels of confidence to determine at what agreement each cell categorised correctly. Hence, it is the reject threshold map which contains the computed index to the calculated confidence level for each classified pixel in the classified raster scene of the satellite image.

\section{Conclusion}

\subsection{Summary}
Current glacier retreat and snow melt in the mountainous regions of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co across Tibetan Plateau have demonstrated increase and fluctuations in extent as climate warming continues to persist over the past ten year (2013 to 2023). If current trends continue then the increase in the increase of bare soil and degraded lands will also continue as a result of glacier retreat, placing more natural land cover types at risk of degrading. Remote sensing data processing using machine learning (ML) techniques implemented in GRASS GIS have proven to be a useful tool in assessing the possible glacial-hydrological impacts these land cover types may have on a regional scale in Lhasa terrain, especially for basin of Lake Nam Co and the surrounding areas.  

\subsection{Perspectives and applications}
Lack of current similar studies that apply machine learning to remote sensing data processing for environmental monitoring of the Qinghai-Tibetan Plateau region motivated this study to contribute to the evaluation of global change effects on the western Chinese ecosystems through mapping land cover types in the study area for comparative analysis of changes resulting from glacier retreat and snow melt in Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains. The combination of remote sensing data analysis and approaches of machine learning methods to data modelling essentially provide  environmental estimates and cartographic visualization in the form of analyse satellite imagery. These data could then be incorporated into future modelling of Tibetan Plateau at the enlarged scale for prediction of environmental changes and hydrological modelling aimed at sustainable development and conservation of alpine high-altitude ecosystems of western China. 

Using scripting techniques and advanced cartographic solutions of GRASS GIS that employ machine learning approach, we identified the periods of glacier retreat and advances detected on a series of the multispectral satellite images. The obtained results help to better understand biennial fluctuations of glacier extent in Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co. Such knowledge can be used for landscape analysis explaining the effects of global warming processes that lead to either a favourable greening of land cover types around the Lake Nam Co or, in contrast, landscape degradation due to the desiccation and aridification of alpine ecosystems of the Tibetan Plateau, China.

\subsection{Future outlooks}

More research needs to be carried out as to how changes in specific land cover types might alter the glaciological-hydrological cycle of other basin of the alpine lake in Tibetan Plateau in order to compare the behaviour of small-sized lakes that may be facing similar effects from global warming resulted in glacier retreat, analyse and compare those outcomes with regard to the Lake Nam Co. More analysis is also required in order to increase both the extent of the land cover classification using a mosaic of images, and to ascertain which specific areas and landscapes of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau would be at most risk of land degradation from global warming that is reflected in the increased snow melt and rates of glacier retreat. This may be possible with a greater availability of higher-resolution remotely sensed data on both spatial and temporal scales (for instance, such as SPOT images) along with larger territorial extent of the analysed dataset as a reference database. 

Both paths are plausible and reasonable given the access to the available datasets with high-resolution imagery covering the target areas of Tibetan Plateau and data processing toolsets. To conclude, we contend that the methodological approaches to which software and data future studies use lies in what extent of the Tibetan Plateau region is selected as a target area. We conclude with suggested strategies of landscape analysis that integrate scripting techniques, machine learning algorithms and satellite image processing can be implemented with geological and topographic data to facilitate a further and more detailed analysis on the complex interrelations between topographic, hydrological and environmental processes as key factors in landscape greening of the Tibetan Plateau, China.
