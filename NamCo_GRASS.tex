\section{Introduction}

Lake Nam Co is the highest and the largest salt high-altitude lake among the thousands of lakes located on the Tibetan Plateau, China. Formed as a result of complex tectonic movements in Himalaya during Tertiary period, it presents now a slightly saline basin located at the elevation of 4,718 m a.s.l. with an average depth of 100 m \cite{Wangetal2009Limnology}. In the late Quaternary period, Lake Nam Co had a vast area and was connected to other small basins surrounding its surface \cite{tb00544}. Old lake terraces elevated up to 28 m above the current lake level evidence that Lake Nam Co gradually decreased in size since the Holocene due to the drying climate starting \cite{Tsui}. 

The surroundings of Lake Nam Co with Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains located in Lhasa terrane, Tibetan Plateau (Qinghai-Tibet Plateau), has experienced rapid glacier retreat over the past 10 years. Snow and ice melting results from the climate warming which includes the rise of air and water temperatures in the region. For instance, \cite{2017JD027379} report the raise of water temperatures in lake over at an approximate rate of 0.5° C during recent four decades since 1979 and high rates of warming for air temperatures over land with 0.2° C. Warming in both mountain glaciers and surface of high-altitude lakes is observed in Tibetan Plateau as a result of global climate-environmental changes and in turn has consequences on the surrounding ecosystems and landscapes \cite{2021JD036320,LIU20216}. Glacier retreat and snow melt is a common occurrence in recent decades due to several unique regional meteorological and characteristics of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and other Tibetan highlands. For instance, changes include the downward trend of snow depth in the high altitude mountains and, in contrast, upward trend of snow depth in the plains of Tibetain Plateaus are reported by \cite{ZHU2022152491}. 

Glacier retreat and snow melt trigger geological and environmental processes in the surroundings areas. As a result of glacial meltwater, the surface area of Lake Nam Co is currently expanding and currently occupies 10,680 km \textsuperscript{2} of catchment area and a total area of 2017 km \textsuperscript{2}. Such expansion in lake's surface results from the hydrological inflow from local rivers and melting glaciers situated in the nearby areas. Thus, Nyainqen Tanglha Mountain Range, located to the south of the Lake Nam Co has a large number of glaciers which meltwater runs into the lake basin through rivers and streams \cite{ZHOU201389}. Recent studies report that glacier meltwater affects the hydrological cycle through redistributed components of water recharge and discharge on the Tibetan Plateau \cite{HE2024130556}. For example, glacier meltwater recharge takes up to 10\% of the total groundwater recharge over the Lhasa River Basin and this percentage increases \cite{CHEN2021100871}. 

One of the main environmental problems in Tibetan Plateau is soil erosion. Through snowmelt and glacier runoff, erosion accelerates the rate of soil losses and destroys upper vegetation layer since the underlying ground surface is abraded of the rock materials, leaving only debris on the bedrock surface  \cite{LI2024107695}. Furthermore, snowmelt and glacier runoff result in deficit of surface water which becomes less available for plants and direct irrigation for agricultural purposes. In turn, it leads to a decrease in natural cover as the surface vegetation. 

Nam Co Lake significantly affects local climate and weather conditions through lower albedos, large heat capacities, and reduced surface roughness. Such effects in processes of lake-air interactions largely impact the distributions of heat fluxes, cumulus clouds, and precipitation which creates local setting for moisture conditions and distribution of lacustrine types of vegetation \cite{2018JD028330}. Hence, the variations in hydrological balance become more essential since the lake belongs to the endorheic basin of Qinghai-Tibet Plateau \cite{Zhuetal2010}. In turn, glacier is highly sensitive to changes in air temperature where positive accumulated air temperature and precipitation level results in changes of glacier surface albedo \cite{Zhouetal2010}. Such interconnected and complex processes results in gradual changes of glacier extent, hydrological regime that affect external surface processes regarding soil and vegetation system on the Lhasa terrain.

Remote sensing data has proved to be a valuable tool for environmental monitoring as a spatio-temporal dynamics \cite{Wangetal2016,Lemenkova202324,Shaoetal2008,Lemenkova202310} and detecting land cover changes in particular \cite{Kulkarni_Bahuguna_2002,jmse11040871}. They allow for rapid and effective data extraction for monitoring and mapping changes in land cover types across diverse areas worldwide. The key approach to processing satellite images is classification which can be performed using unsupervised (automatic) and supervised methods. The main idea behind these methods lies in grouping pixels on the satellite images together so that they form clusters, or groups of pixels, with similar properties of spectral reflectance. The machine algorithms recognise similarities in spectral signatures of these pixels across all the bans and assigns them into classes during classification process \cite{Lemenkova202321}. The classes may be categorised then into predefined land cover types identified on the study area. Once the classification is achieved and the accuracy assessment is completed, the maps obtained on the basis of the classified images are used for comparative analysis to analyse changes that take place over the specific study area for each land cover type that has taken place between the target time periods \cite{CHEN2007298,Lemenkova202323,LIU2021104942}.

Despite the necessary geographical and environmental conditions being in place for catastrophic potential of glacier retreat across the  Lake Nam Co areas, regional scale studies on comparative analysis of land cover changes caused by glacier retreat and snow melt are still limited for Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains. There are also many lacustrine basins in the region of Lhasa terrain region, that do not have received attention to monitor the size or rate of glacier retreat. At the same time, investigating changes in river and lake hydrology over Tibetan Plateau is perspective using satellite remote sensing data to analyse basin-wide land cover changes. Using remote sensing imagery processed by machine learning methods, this article examines the changes in land cover types over the ten-year period of 2013-2023 in Lake Nam Co and Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains, Tibetan Plateau, China.

\section{Data}

The United States Geological Survey (USGS) provided the necessary satellite imagery to perform, visualise and map land cover analysis with 30-m resolution multispectral Landsat 8-9 Operational Land Imager (OLI) and Thermal Infrared (TIRS) remotely sensed images. The satellite images cover study areas of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co for six dates in cold period with high level of snow/glacier extent: 31 December 2013, 19 November 2015, 10 December 2017, 16 December 2019, 21 December 2021, 03 December 2023. No quality images were available for December 2015 for comparison with other images (the available scenes had high cloudiness and distortion of visibility), and so the Landsat OLI/TIRS image for 19 November 2015 was used in place of the available but highly-cloudy image for 21 December 2015. The requirement for cloudiness level was set up on lesser than 10\% for all the Landsat images. These time periods reflected a significant variations in glacier and snow coverage over the ranges of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains which shows the development of land cover types after snow and glacier melt, and hydrological-environmental setting of Nyenchen Tanglha Mountains and Lake Nam Co, Tibetan Plateau. The dataset is shown in Figure \ref{fig02}.

Along with the similarity in seasonal timing (winter period of December and November) necessary for comparing the differences in land cover types, the Landsat OLI/TIRS images had also already been ortho-rectified and corrected using Landsat Collection Level 1 corrections for terrain to allow for direct comparison of the imagery. All the data are predefined and projected using Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) Zone 46 for western part of the People's Republic of China (P.R.C.). 

Basin-wide topographic raster data in digital format were available from the General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans (GEBCO), NetCDF format, available from the \href{https://www.gebco.net/}{https://www.gebco.net/} GEBCO organisational website, which provided the necessary terrain reference data for the evaluation of relief of the surroundings.
